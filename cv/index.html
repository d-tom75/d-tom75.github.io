Publications

  • 2025.09.20
    Studying Domain Shifts in Bioacoustic Recording Style With Information Theory (Preprint)
    Available at SSRN (Preprint No. 5510368)
    A major challenge in machine learning (ML) for studying passive acoustic monitoring is the domain shift between directional, handheld, well-labeled focal audio data and omnidirectional, passively recorded, under-labeled soundscapes. ML models trained on the vastly more labeled focal recording datasets struggle to generalize when tested on soundscape recordings. Research in this area has typically focused on the methodology behind training ML models, both to study and alleviate the domain shift. However, less work has been done in quantifying the domain shift hypothesis beyond model performance and species density. This potentially limits our understanding of the factors behind domain shift. We find that the Ecoacoustic Global Complexity Index (EGCI), an acoustic complexity index rooted in information theory, can effectively describe the domain shift and assist in productive research of this domain shift. We demonstrate EGCI's effectiveness in three experiments. First, we show that an SVM can easily classify between soundscape and focal recordings using only EGCI values, with an average accuracy of 81.08% (minimum approx 65%). Second, we demonstrate that EGCI, in conjunction with species density via linear regression, can predict soundscape performance and reveal that EGCI values are negatively correlated with soundscape loss. Finally, we demonstrate that data augmentation, shown in the literature to alleviate domain shift in model performance experiments, reduces the KL-divergence between the EGCI distributions of focal recordings and soundscape recordings.
